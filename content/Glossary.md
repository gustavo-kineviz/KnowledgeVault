## **LLM**
A type of AI (usually a Transformer-based neural network) trained on **vast amounts of text/code data**. It learns patterns, semantics, and world knowledge to predict the next word in a sequence—enabling text generation, translation, coding, and more.

Unlike rule-based systems, it’s a **"black box"** that learns implicitly. Excels at natural language but can’t reason or "understand" like humans.

## **Anterograde Amnesia**
A memory disorder where new memories **can’t be formed** after the condition begins.

_Metaphor for LLMs_: They "forget" earlier parts of a conversation once the input exceeds their context window. Unlike humans, they don’t _experience_ forgetting—it’s a hardware limitation.  
![[Pasted image 20250623232140.png]]

When the concept is applied to Large Language Models (LLMs), it refers to specific limitations in their ability to remember or retain new information, particularly beyond their initial training and within a conversation. It's a metaphor, as LLMs don't have brains or conscious experiences of forgetting like humans do.

Once a conversation or input exceeds this window, the LLM effectively "forgets" the earlier parts of the conversation. It can't refer back to information that has scrolled out of its current processing scope.
